When you request parts on a ticket, the office orders them and marks them received when they arrive. Your **Dashboard** tells you when yours are ready so you can grab them before a job.

## Asking for faster shipping

When you request a part, there's a **Shipping** choice: **Standard**, **2nd Day Air**, or **Next Day Air**. Leave it on Standard unless the customer actually needs it sooner — that's what most parts ship as.

Pick 2nd Day or Next Day and a note box appears. Use it for anything the buyer needs to know when they place the order, like *"customer's UPS account"* or *"must land before Friday"*.

Whatever you pick shows up on the part in the office's Parts Queue, so the person placing the order sees it before they choose a carrier. A rush request only works if you make it **when you request the part** — once the PO is placed, it's too late.

> Faster shipping usually costs more, and the office bills that freight to the customer as a **Shipping** line on the work order. Only ask for it when the customer wants it.

## What you see

On your dashboard, in the items that need attention, look for **My Parts Ready for Pickup**. It shows a count of parts that have arrived and are waiting for you. Tap the card to see the details.

You'll only see **your own** parts — the ones tied to tickets assigned to you.

## How the parts flow works

1. You request a part on a PM or service ticket — including the shipping speed you want.
2. The office orders it (it's "on order").
3. When it arrives, the office marks it **received** — and it shows up in your **Ready for Pickup** count.

## Tips

- Check this before heading to a job that's waiting on parts — no wasted trips.
- A ticket won't let you mark it complete until its ordered parts are received, so if you're blocked, this is the place to confirm whether the part has landed yet.
